Relevant document IDs are only one part of a successful answer. This diagnostic
asks where the remaining gap arises: in the generator's use of evidence, in
the reference material, or in the judge's interpretation.
We examine both saved practice arms (350 queries each), then uniformly sample
20 of Qwen's 121 three-dimension-zero answers without replacement from sorted
IDs using Python \texttt{random.Random(20261118)}.
The case review uses saved questions, answers, judge reasons and source texts.
It is AI-assisted and unblinded; the authors subsequently confirmed three
representative evidence chains. This phase uses no dev answers or new model/API calls.

\subsection{Evidence must retain its subject, scope and conditions}

\textbf{A company-tax rule becomes a pension claim.}
For the Ireland pension question (\texttt{q\_371c6ca10bf1bf5e}), both gold
records are present alongside UK/US pension and company-tax material.
Record \texttt{r\_dc4b5d2c6048003d} says ``there will be a tax loss that can be
carried forward and offset against future profits''.
The answer changes the subject: ``There are maximum contribution limits, but
these can be carried forward and offset against future profits''.
The text is traceable to retrieved evidence, yet its application changes from
a company's tax loss to pension contributions.
It preserves the wording while changing what the rule applies to.

\textbf{Correct numbers can lose their conditions.}
In the Tangerine transfer question (\texttt{q\_66bb08d4774227f7}), a short gold
document ranks first and states that funds arrive 2--3 business days after
the recipient completes the required actions. The answer preserves the number
but omits those actions as the trigger.
In the crisis-assets case (\texttt{q\_e86dfaec0aa23a50}), it changes gold/silver's
usefulness from \emph{after} the crisis to \emph{during} it.
Both examples show why a relevant fact needs its conditions.
Extracting only a keyword-bearing sentence could lose precisely that information.

Two further cases illustrate selection of the wrong point within the context.
The stocks-on-sale answer (\texttt{q\_1024d33a7dfa33ef}) imports ``Written
Confirmation'' from a share-disposal/tax-basis document as a stock-selection
factor. The car-financing answer (\texttt{q\_ce474d8f8d548797}) combines general
cash/loan advice while omitting the supplied point that dealers care about
immediate, risk-free payment. The latter question is brief enough to allow
broader interpretations, so that diagnosis remains ambiguous.

\subsection{Evaluation adds a separate source of uncertainty}

The generator and judge see different evidence sets. Generation uses the
retrieved top five; judging is instructed to use only gold documents.
For example, the expense-ratio answer (\texttt{q\_0a12138b81eb7e2a}) mentions
quarterly reports and prospectuses supported by retrieved text outside gold.
Reference-only scoring can therefore penalise claims with retrieved support.
That support still needs checking for factual accuracy and applicability.

\textbf{A judge reason can misdescribe its evidence.}
For the software question (\texttt{q\_e16177bbeb991130}), the answer includes
gold's MetaStock, Incredible Charts and VectorVest recommendations.
It also repeats Bloomberg wording from a retrieved non-gold document.
The judge calls that wording unsupported, then attributes the same wording to
gold. The source comparison identifies a problem in the explanation's account
of evidence; it does not resolve the appropriate score for the whole answer.

In the crisis-assets case, alongside the answer's
\emph{after}/\emph{during} mistake, the judge denies
helicopters and connections that the gold texts actually contain.
Generator and judge errors coexist: finding one does not erase the other.
The remaining flagged judge reasons concern the eBay abstention, attribution
to credit bureaus versus creditors, Tangerine timing wording, and
personal-versus-business tax framing. All six flags have source-linked records.

\textbf{The zeros come from recorded judge decisions.}
All 700 saved answers are nonempty and finish with \texttt{stop}; their scores
match cached results and valid raw judge responses.
Malformed scores trigger parser errors, and exhausted retries raise an error
rather than silently supplying zero. Within each arm, the zero-ID sets for
correctness, grounding and completeness coincide: 138 for dense and 121 for
Qwen, with no partially zero dimension vectors.
The paired zero/nonzero counts are 85/53/36/176 for
zero--zero/zero--positive/positive--zero/positive--positive: 53 leave zero and
36 enter it. This describes changes in judge decisions, not verified accuracy.

The synchronized dimensions raise a calibration question.
The frozen judge source, checked modulo LF/CRLF line endings, uses the format
example \texttt{\{"correctness":0,"grounding":0,"completeness":0,\ldots\}}.
Value copying or anchoring is a plausible prompt-design risk, but no alternate
prompt was tested. Its contribution cannot be separated here from strict
reference-only scoring, real answer defects and other judge behaviour.

\textbf{Scope of the conclusions.}
The counts describe the inspected Qwen practice judge-zero sample rather than
all queries, all errors or dev. Likewise, 30/121 is the fraction of Qwen zeros
with full coverage, not the zero rate among all full-coverage queries.
Conditioning separately on each arm's zeros prevents a causal comparison of
their evidence-use failure rates.
Source tracing establishes the reported text mismatches, while explaining their
causes would require controlled intervention.
No document-removal or snippet benefit was measured, and the descriptive
conclusions do not depend on adding such an experiment.
All original scores and paired intervals are retained.
