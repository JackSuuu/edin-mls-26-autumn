3. **Intrinsic Value and Time Value**: Options are generally viewed as having two types of value: intrinsic value (based on the difference between the strike price and the spot price of the underlying) and time value (based on the volatility of the underlying and the time left until expiration).

4. **Delta and Theta**: These are "Greeks" used to measure the sensitivity of an option's price to changes in the underlying asset's price and the passage of time, respectively.

5. **State Prices/Risk-Neutral Probabilities**: If you have two other assets with known payoffs and prices, you can value the option using state prices or risk-neutral probabilities.

6. **Bootstrapping the Yield Curve**: Although not directly related to options, this method is used to construct a yield curve, which can be relevant in certain financial contexts.
